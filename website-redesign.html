Website redesign —
keep what works, rebuild the rest

Your site exists, it still works, but it is showing its age: slow on mobile, awkward to update, invisible in AI answers, and no longer a reflection of what you have become. A redesign is not a new build with an old site in the way — it is inheritance work: knowing what to keep, what to drop, and above all what must not break.

What is preserved

  • The search visibility you earned — the URLs that bring traffic are recorded first, kept where possible, permanently redirected where they change.
  • Your content — texts, images, listings, articles: what took work is carried over, not rewritten for the sake of it.
  • Your data — accounts, subscribers, history: migrated where they exist, with a check before switching over.
  • Your email addresses and domain name — continuity comes before aesthetics.

What actually changes

A useful redesign is not only visible on screen. The site becomes fast on a phone over 4G, updates without depending on anyone, complies with GDPR without stacking banners, and above all becomes readable by search engines and by AI assistants: semantic structure, structured data, content that answers the questions people actually ask. That is the difference between a site that gets found and a site that only exists for those who already know its address.

And where it fits the need, a redesign is the right moment to build in useful AI — a conversational assistant grounded in your own content, semantic search — rather than bolting it on later.

How it works

First an assessment: what the current site does well, what it does badly, what it brings in. Then a straight call between redesigning and rebuilding — both have a cost, and it is not always the one you expect. Then iterative design, content migration, the redirect plan, and a supported launch with search visibility checked in the following weeks.

The feasibility questionnaire has a dedicated redesign path: current URL, what to do with the site, content to carry over, data to migrate, ranking to preserve, credentials available. Ten minutes are enough to get an honest reading within 48 business hours.

Frequently asked questions

Should everything be rebuilt, or can the existing site be kept?

Both happen. When the structure and the content still hold, the look, the speed and the search visibility are rebuilt while keeping the architecture. When the technical foundation is exhausted, rebuilding often costs less than patching. The feasibility questionnaire settles that point up front.

Will I lose my Google ranking?

Not if the redesign is done properly. Existing URLs and their traffic are recorded first, the addresses that work are kept, and permanent redirects are set up for those that change. It is the part of a redesign most often rushed, and the only one that keeps costing afterwards.

What happens to my content, images and data?

They are carried over: texts, images, listings, articles, and data where there is any (accounts, subscribers, orders). The questionnaire asks about each of these so that nothing is discovered halfway through.

I no longer have access to my current site — is that a blocker?

No. It is common when a site was left with a provider who is no longer reachable. A redesign can be carried out without the historical credentials, provided the domain name can be recovered — a point checked during scoping.
